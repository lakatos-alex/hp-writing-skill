# Test Run B: Skill-Aided (No Books) — English

## Scene

In the corridor outside the library, Tobias balanced on his upturned satchel, fingers two inches short of the high ventilation grille. Caught behind the flaking iron scrollwork was a folded square of parchment: Professor McGonagall's signed permission for the reserve shelves, sucked through the bars by a draught.

"We could just Summon it," Tobias muttered, heels sliding on the brass buckles.

Lina braced a boot against the bag. "We haven't learned the Summoning Charm, Tobias."

"It's only *Accio*," he said, stretching until his sleeves rode up. "Flitwick had the fourth-years doing it on Tuesday."

"And Professor Flitwick said we'd pull down half the plaster if we tried it without the theory," Lina said. "Get down. Madam Pince won't hold that commentary on Switching Spells past six."

Tobias hopped down with a thud. If the slip was not on the desk before the dinner bell, his reserve lapsed to a fifth-year Ravenclaw, leaving him without citations for tomorrow's Transfiguration essay.

Lina drew her wand. The grille was seven feet up, but first-year Charms still worked on paper. Aiming through the diamond fretwork, she swished and flicked. "*Wingardium Leviosa*."

The slip lifted from the sill, floating smoothly toward the gap. But Tobias, eager to snare it, thrust his own wand between the scrolls to guide the corner. His tip struck an internal catch.

A heavy metallic clack echoed down the corridor. The flue's counterweight dropped inside the wall, snapping the iron damper shut. The parchment jerked to a halt, its top third pinched fast in the seam.

From the clock tower, the first chime of the evening bell rolled through the stone. Across the gallery, Madam Pince appeared in the library doorway, reaching for the heavy oak doors.

A soft scrape sounded along the flags.

Mrs. Norris padded around the corner, yellow, lamp-like eyes fixed on Tobias's bag. Argus Filch emerged behind her from the shadow of the one-eyed witch statue, dragging his bad foot and rattling a heavy key ring.

"Tampering with castle fixtures," Filch wheezed, jowls shaking with sour triumph. "Out of bounds after the warning bell."

The bell struck its third stroke. Madam Pince was swinging the first door shut.

Waiting for Filch meant confiscation, detention, and a lost book. The only alternative was what hung within reach.

"The signature's trapped inside," Tobias said, freezing as Filch closed the distance. "She won't take it without the—"

Lina did not debate. She seized the dangling end of the parchment and pulled.

The sheet tore with a dry rip. The header remained clamped in the grille; the bottom scrap, bearing only Tobias's ink-blotted name, came free in her hand. She shoved it into Tobias's chest and pushed him toward the doors.

"Run!"

Tobias bolted. He slid beneath Madam Pince's arm just as the heavy oak slammed into its frame.

Lina turned back. Filch's bony fingers fastened into the collar of her robes, smelling of carbolic soap and damp stone. Through the glass pane of the door, Lina watched Madam Pince hold the torn fragment up to her spectacles, shake her head once with thin-lipped finality, and slide the reserved textbook beneath the counter.

"Right," Filch rasped, giving her shoulder an unkind shake. "Two hours scrubbing chamber pots before you see a bite of supper."
