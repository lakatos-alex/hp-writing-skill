## 1. Finished Scene

The permission slip was wedged seven feet up, pinned behind the blackened iron scrollwork of an old heating grille. Through the bars, Lina could just read Professor Flitwick’s cheerful purple handwriting: *Permitted: T. Finch, Reserve Volume.*

‘We could use a Summoning Charm,’ Tobias said, rocking on his heels. He still smelled faintly of crushed beetles from afternoon Potions. ‘Point, say *Accio*, job done.’

‘We’re in third year,’ Lina said tartly. ‘Miranda Goshawk doesn’t introduce Summoning Charms until *Grade Four*. Flitwick hasn’t taught us, and you can’t even do a proper Softening Charm.’

‘Madam Pince said if this slip isn’t logged before the dinner gong, she’s giving the reserve copy of *Intermediate Transfiguration* to Roger Davies,’ Tobias hissed, misery pinching his freckled face. ‘I’ve two rolls to write on Switching Spells by morning. McGonagall will give me a zero.’

‘Then be quiet and let me aim.’

Lina drew her wand, breathed in the October chill leaking from the high arrow-slits, and aimed through a diamond in the grille. First-year work. Swish and flick.

‘*Wingardium Leviosa.*’

The slip lifted cleanly from the ledge, floating like a pale moth toward the bars. Tobias gasped with hope—and then a whistling draught from the chimney flue caught the parchment. It spun, fluttering wildly, and caught hard on a rusted iron tooth on the damper latch.

The iron plate groaned beneath the strain, tilting open above a vertical drop into the castle foundations. If Lina released the charm, the slip would drop into the soot forever; if she tugged gently, the tooth would hold fast.

Then, from around the corner, came the soft, unmistakable patter of paws.

Two lamp-like yellow eyes slid into the torchlight. Mrs Norris stopped, dust-coloured coat bristling, fixing them with an unblinking glare. A second later came the dragging scrape of boots.

‘What’s this?’ wheezed Argus Filch, shuffling into view, his lantern throwing sickly yellow light across the stones. ‘Lurking after the bell? Poking castle fittings?’

He was ten paces away. With no time to finesse the spell, Lina had to choose: lose the pass entirely, or tear it to get them moving before Filch seized her wand. She wrenched her wrist sharply sideways.

The charm snapped the parchment across the middle. The top half—carrying Flitwick’s purple flourish—shot through the bars into her hand. The bottom half vanished down the black shaft.

Filch bore down on them, Mrs Norris weaving malevolently around his ankles. ‘Wands out, is it? Tampering with the heating flues—detentions, both of you—’

‘We dropped our library slip, Mr Filch,’ Lina said quickly, thrusting the torn parchment forward before Tobias could stammer. ‘We were on our way to the Great Hall.’

Filch snatched the scrap, squinting under his lantern, his jowls quivering with disappointment. ‘Ragged rubbish. Madam Pince won’t honour half a ticket. Get down to the Hall before I lock the gates and leave you out for the Dementors.’

They sprinted for the library instead, skidding to the heavy oak doors just as Madam Pince was turning the great brass key.

The librarian inspected the torn, sooty scrap with the thin disdain of an underfed vulture. ‘Half a slip is not a loan, Finch. The book remains here. You have thirty minutes to copy your notes at the counter before I lock up.’

She shut the door on Lina. Outside in the draughty corridor, Lina listened to the muffled, miserable squeak of Tobias’s quill, while the distant dinner gong echoed from below, summoning everyone else to hot roast beef.
