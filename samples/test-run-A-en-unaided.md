## Scene

The decorative iron grille was set eight feet up the third-floor corridor wall, and behind its lattice, the library permission slip fluttered like a trapped moth.

"Just Summon it," Tobias muttered, shifting nervously. "A quick *Accio* and we’re done."

"We're in third year, Tobias," Lina said, glaring up at the iron scrollwork. "Professor Flitwick hasn't taught us the Summoning Charm yet. Do you want your wand to backfire and singe your eyebrows off?"

"Flitwick wouldn't expel us," Tobias insisted. His panic was entirely practical: he had sworn to return the signed slip before the dinner bell. If he failed, Madam Pince would bar him from the library for a fortnight, leaving him without *Intermediate Transfiguration* to finish Professor McGonagall’s essay on Switching Spells.

"Stand back," Lina sighed, drawing her wand. "A simple Levitation Charm will do."

She aimed through the metalwork, swished, and flicked. *"Wingardium Leviosa."*

The slip rose obediently, but Lina hadn't accounted for the fierce October draft rushing through the flue. The parchment shot upward, spun wildly, and jammed against an internal flange caked in greasy soot. Worse, the stray lift rattled the loose iron grate with a sharp, echoing *clang*.

Lina froze. Down the corridor padded Mrs. Norris, her lamp-like yellow eyes fixing unblinkingly on Lina's raised wand. A heartbeat later, the wheezing scrape of Argus Filch’s boots echoed against the flagstones.

The caretaker rounded the corner, clutching an oily rag, his jowls quivering. "Tampering with castle fittings?" he wheezed. "Wands out in the corridor?"

"Dropped a quill, Mr. Filch," Lina said smoothly, slipping her wand into her sleeve while Tobias stared at his boots in guilty silence.

Filch sneered, Mrs. Norris weaving around his ankles. "Five minutes till dinner. If you aren't seated in the Great Hall when the gong sounds, it's detention. Scrubbing chamberpots without magic." He shuffled off, leaving the faint stench of vinegar and dust.

The warning bell faded, leaving barely three minutes before Madam Pince locked the library desk. High in the vent, the parchment hung by a single blackened corner.

Lina’s charm had wedged it tight. She faced an impossible choice: spend two painstaking minutes coaxing it loose with delicate wandwork, missing the dinner bell and earning Filch's detention, or yank the charm down hard right now, tearing the parchment free but ripping Professor McGonagall’s signature in half.

"Yank it!" Tobias hissed, watching the corridor. "A torn slip is better than detention!"

Lina chose. She whipped her wand downward with a sharp flick.

The slip tore with a dry snap. Half fluttered into Lina's hand, blackened with soot and severed beneath McGonagall’s sign-off; the counter-signature remained stuck fast in the grille.

They sprinted down the passage, skidding outside the library just as Madam Pince drew the oak doors shut.

"Madam Pince, wait!" Tobias panted, thrusting the ragged scrap forward.

The librarian peered down her vulture-like nose at the mutilated scrap. Her nostrils flared.

"An unsigned, defaced remnant?" Madam Pince said coldly. "The reserve shelves remain locked, Mr. Higgins."

With a sharp snap, the brass bolt slid home. Lina stared at her soot-stained fingers, then at Tobias's fallen face, as the great dinner gong began to boom across the castle.
