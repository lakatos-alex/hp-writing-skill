## Research brief

Assuming Harry’s fifth year, **1995–96**, the investigation works with adjustments. Later books establish constraints for the author; students need their own route to that knowledge.

| Premise | Book evidence and limits | Useful story option |
|---|---|---|
| **The Room conjures dinner** | **DH15, “The Goblin’s Revenge”:** Hermione explains that food cannot be made from nothing; existing food can be summoned, transformed or increased. **DH29, “The Lost Diadem”:** Neville reports that hunger prompted a passage to Aberforth, who supplies food. The Room supplies access, not meals from nothing. | Have students bring kitchen provisions; **GoF21** shows food obtained from the kitchens for a party. A newly discovered supply passage is a plausible **story invention**, but the later Aberforth arrangement is not automatically available in fifth year. |
| **A laptop works there** | **GoF28, “The Madness of Mr Crouch”:** Hermione, citing *Hogwarts: A History*, says computers and other electrical equipment malfunction around Hogwarts. **OotP18** demonstrates the Room supplying training equipment, but establishes no exception for electronics. | Use parchment files and a physical clue board. A functioning laptop requires an explicitly invented shielding mechanism or magical adaptation; merely asking the Room does not establish a canonical workaround. |
| **Reading a memory proves guilt** | **OotP24, “Occlumency”:** Snape describes Legilimency as conditional extraction and interpretation, with Occlumency providing resistance. **HBP17, “A Sluggish Memory”:** Slughorn’s displayed recollection is visibly altered; Dumbledore also reconstructs how Morfin received a false memory and confessed. **HBP23, “Horcruxes”:** the authentic Slughorn memory reveals the concealed conversation. | A memory can provide decisive evidence, but establish its origin, access and corroboration. Let it reveal a hiding place or checkable detail. Alternatively, the apparently incriminating memory becomes the clue that someone framed the suspect. Pensieve access and advanced Legilimency should each have a credible route. |

**Would refusing a Triwizard task destroy a champion’s magical core?**  
There is **no book-canon basis for that penalty**. Dumbledore describes a binding magical contract in **GoF16, “The Goblet of Fire”**; Crouch insists selected champions must compete in **GoF17, “The Four Champions.”** Neither specifies what refusal causes.

Failure is demonstrably possible: Fleur fails to retrieve her hostage and receives points in **GoF26, “The Second Task.”** **GoF31, “The Third Task,”** explicitly provides rescue by red sparks; Harry understands that this means disqualification from the task. These examples do **not** establish that outright refusal is consequence-free. Ron’s death penalty for breaking an **Unbreakable Vow** in **HBP16** concerns a different mechanism.

For your story, use uncertainty and adult pressure, a genuine attempt followed by rescue, or an explicitly invented refusal penalty. Magical-core destruction would be **AU/fanon**, not an established consequence.

**Suggested investigation:** kitchen provisions sustain clandestine meetings; paper records organise clues; a suspicious memory points to physical evidence; obtaining corroboration exposes the investigators to discovery. This preserves the Room’s usefulness without making it an automatic solution.
