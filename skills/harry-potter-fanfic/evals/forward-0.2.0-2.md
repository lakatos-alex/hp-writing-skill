Mara unlocked the workshop with Ivo’s brass key and caught the door before it struck the bell. The bell rang anyway. She had repaired it too well.

Ivo stood at the bench, tipping screws from a school metronome into a saucer.

“It accelerates whenever someone makes a mistake,” he said.

“An educational philosophy in a box.”

“I’ve been outvoted by it twice.”

She hung her coat beside his. Their sleeves touched; she moved hers to the next peg, then felt ridiculous.

Through the window came the scrape of someone removing the last anniversary bunting. A year since the war, and the street still couldn’t agree whether taking decorations down was disrespectful. Sirius Black had written something pointed about that in yesterday’s Prophet. Mara had read it while avoiding the narrow case beneath her bench.

She laid the key beside the saucer.

“Hold the pendulum upright.”

Ivo steadied it between two fingers. Mara loosened the backplate. Inside, a correction charm glowed an unhealthy orange around the escapement.

“Someone’s tightened this to compensate for a worn spring.”

“Can you undo it?”

“Yes. It’ll still need a spring.”

She touched her wand to the little brass wheel. The orange light dwindled; the pendulum kicked against Ivo’s fingers.

“Let go.”

He did. Three screws rose from the saucer.

“Not you,” she told them, and pressed them down with her palm.

Ivo laughed. She wanted another minute of that. She could have it, too: ask about his pupils, complain about suppliers, put the kettle on. The case would remain where it was.

He picked up the worn spring.

“Shall I put this with the parts?”

“Keep it beside the mechanism. Otherwise we’ll forget what we’ve changed.”

Her hand stopped on the screwdriver.

She set it down.

“Ivo. I broke the instrument in the narrow case.”

He looked towards the space beneath her bench.

“The concertina?”

“Yes.”

“What happened?”

“I tried to free the seized endplate without releasing the tension. The mounting split. Two reeds snapped.”

He laid the spring down carefully.

“When?”

“Tuesday.”

It was Friday. The workshop clock supplied several unnecessary seconds.

“I thought I could mend it before you—” She stopped. “I wanted to tell you after I’d made it less bad.”

“Bring it out.”

She crouched, pulled the case clear and lifted it onto the bench. There was dust on one corner. She wiped it away with her sleeve, a small, useless improvement.

Ivo opened the catches himself.

Mara had wrapped the loose pieces in cream felt. He unfolded it, then rested a finger beside the split wood without touching the break.

“My mother used to catch her cuff on that screw.”

“I know it was hers. You told me when you brought it in.”

He looked at her then.

“And you let me come here yesterday and ask how it was going.”

“Yes.”

“You said it needed time.”

“I’m sorry.”

He shut his eyes briefly. Mara kept her hands against the bench edge. Reaching for him would give him another thing to deal with.

“Can you mend it?”

“The mounting, probably. The reeds will need replacing. I can’t promise it’ll sound exactly the same.”

“I don’t want you experimenting until it does.”

“All right.”

“I mean it, Mara.”

She nodded.

He took his spectacles off and cleaned them with the hem of his jumper. One lens remained smeared. She watched him put them back on.

“I’ll find someone who repairs these,” he said. “Someone else.”

“I can give you names.”

“I’ll ask at the music shop.”

That hurt. She made herself leave it alone.

He reached across the saucer and pocketed the brass key.

“I’d like to be here when the case is opened.”

“All right.”

For a while he examined the pieces. Then he moved the metronome aside.

“Have you got paper? I want everything counted before it goes anywhere.”

She fetched a sheet and a shallow tray. At his direction she laid out the fragments, leaving space between them. He numbered each piece on the paper while she sketched its position.

At the bottom she wrote: Two reeds broken. Mounting split. No further work carried out.

“Date it,” Ivo said.

She added Tuesday’s date beside the damage, and today’s beside the inventory.

He checked both. Then he held the felt open while she lifted the first fragment into the tray.
