# Chapter production

Use for drafting, continuation and substantial expansion. Begin with the previous accepted scene and the requested beats, language, length, POV and emotional destination.

## Prepare enough to sustain length

A long chapter needs several developments. Allocate rough space to arrival or opening pressure, interaction, complication, consequential choice and aftermath; adapt to the story. These are planning functions, not mandatory visible sections or fixed percentages.

For each scene, identify who wants what now, why the request is difficult, what the viewpoint character notices, and what changes. If two adjacent scenes perform the same function, differentiate their pressure or combine them. Include quiet time when it changes familiarity, interpretation or willingness to act.

## Draft from particular activity

Give dialogue a surface task: repairing a cupboard, preparing a lesson, walking through a busy corridor, returning borrowed equipment, cleaning up after visitors. Activity sets pace, creates interruptions and makes care visible. The magic should affect the task rather than decorate it.

Use physical geography at moments of consequence: doors, distance, who can overhear, what is within reach, where an injured person can rest. Avoid continuous room tours. The viewpoint selects details according to present concern; a nervous guest and a caretaker notice different things.

## Continuation and expansion

Preserve the exact accepted state at the handoff. A new chapter cannot quietly restore lost equipment, knowledge withheld from a character, or a relationship agreement not yet reached.

When enlarging a chapter, add consequential material: a disagreement with a cost, another person's initiative, a practical obstacle, an earned memory with a present trigger, or aftermath. Do not repeat reassurance, attraction or atmosphere to meet a number. Required beats should have enough room to affect later choices.

If a requested change needs a new magical ability, use the magic workflow to establish prerequisites and limits. If the scene's central fact is disputed, research it before building the whole chapter around it.

## Voice and dialogue

Calibrate to approved project passages. Preserve narrative distance, humour, rhythm, imagery and conventions of address. Distinguish a character's objective from a speech habit. Let exchanges contain incomplete answers, changed tactics, directness and silence; not every line needs subtext.

For intimacy, give participants independent initiative, preferences and boundaries. Affection should be specific to their history. Preserve the requested rating and actual ages; a fade can carry emotional continuity into the following scene.

## Save and verify

Use the project's established filename and format. Measure the saved text using its counting method, or the optional audit command when no method exists. Check required beats, chronology, possessions, injuries and information transfers against the actual draft. Update existing notes with accepted events, not discarded planning possibilities.

Deliver the written artifact and concise handoff. State actual count if requested. If the target was missed, revise toward it; do not rename a shorter result as the requested length.
