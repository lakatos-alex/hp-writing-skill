# Goblet of Fire: spectacle and institutional failure

## Narrative orientation

GoF1–9 links private danger to a large public event and its aftermath. GoF10–17 introduces the Tournament and coerced participation. GoF18–31 interleaves tasks, research, social conflict, publicity and unseen intervention. GoF32–37 moves from catastrophe to testimony, denial, grief and decisions about the future.

## Public and private plots

A competition provides a visible calendar while another actor manipulates the conditions. Distinguish what a task appears to test from who supplies the information or materials that allow it to be completed. Help can be sincere, coerced, manipulated or misinterpreted.

Harry's selection changes peer relationships and public reputation before the first task. Ron's hurt and later repair should be read in sequence. Cedric has competence and ethical choices before becoming a victim; his death should not erase that personhood.

The Yule Ball has independent social stakes. It can expose jealousy and incompatible assumptions without needing to solve the main investigation.

## Authority and evidence

GoF17's contract is asserted as binding, but a detailed penalty system is not supplied. The impersonator's teaching in GoF14 cannot be attributed to the real Moody as a personal history of classroom behaviour.

GoF35 gives a confession under Veritaserum; GoF36 demonstrates that acquiring information does not guarantee an institution will preserve, believe or act on it. A political story needs this gap between evidence and legitimate public action.

## World details worth retrieving

- GoF6–9: Portkeys, international travel, the campsite, secrecy work and violence.
- GoF14: the three curses and apparent Moody's teaching.
- GoF17: Tournament obligations.
- GoF21: kitchens, Dobby's wages and Winky's distress.
- GoF23: celebration, appearance and social misunderstandings.
- GoF26: the lake task and Dobby's intervention.
- GoF28: Hermione's explanation of electrical interference.
- GoF35–37: confession, denial and aftermath.

## Craft transfer

Use a public schedule to create deadlines, attention and constraints while private plans develop. An antagonist's intervention should have a causal route; hidden influence is not permission for unlimited foresight.

Social scenes can alter who will cooperate later. A damaged friendship changes research access, confidence and willingness to ask for help. Repair is a plot event when it changes what the group can do.

After a climax, let institutions and relationships respond. Testimony, disbelief, logistics, mourning and financial decisions can carry substantial narrative weight.

## Continuation questions

At each point, separate the reader's suspicions, Harry's beliefs, what the impersonator knows, what Dumbledore can establish and what Fudge is willing to acknowledge. A story set after the return cannot assume official agreement simply because the reader witnessed it.
