# Context and evidence management

Use for long serial projects, multi-book investigations and bilingual comparison. A short scene rarely needs this whole procedure.

## Keep three layers separate

| Layer | Contents | Update when |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted story state | Date, POV, canon cutoff, divergences, names, possession, knowledge, relationship changes, promises | A story decision is accepted |
| Evidence packet | Decisive chapter anchors, short paraphrases, speaker, time, qualification, unresolved conflict | A claim is investigated |
| Writing brief | Present want, resistance, scene turn, emotional stakes, desired language/register, length | A scene is drafted or revised |

Use the host's existing records. These are roles for information, not three mandatory new files. A source claim does not silently become a story decision; a draft possibility does not silently become accepted continuity.

## Retrieve to answer a question

Start with the relevant workflow, preceding scene and story state. Ask what unknown could materially alter the result: an information route, a spell limit, a name, or the meaning of a confrontation. Use the evidence cards or glossary to locate it. Read its surrounding passage when local books are available. Broaden to neighbouring chapters or a whole arc when the first passage leaves a real ambiguity.

Use a concrete stopping question: can this character know the fact, can the spell do the required work, or is this the intended Hungarian referent? Once the decisive context answers it, retain a brief paraphrase and locator and return to drafting. Reopen evidence if a later decision exposes a new uncertainty. A source read that changes nothing may still confirm a choice, but do not claim it improved the prose merely because it occurred.

For simple drafting, start with one workflow. Retrieve a glossary entry for a term, a prose section for a voice problem, or a scene-state check for a consequential transfer. Reserve broader dossiers and cross-book reading for questions that need them. This is a starting policy, not a hard context cap: a difficult scene may require more evidence.

For Hungarian translation choices, align by stable chapter anchor. Different languages have different sentence order and length; the same character offset is not the same passage. Search separately for the referent, examine each context, then record the choice. A lexical match establishes presence, not a correct translation or a rule's universality.

If books are absent, the bundled dossiers, facts and terminology support planning and drafting. Use their limits. For an exact quotation, a disputed translation, or a detail outside those records, state the gap or ask for the passage instead of treating fluent recollection as verification. Do not stop an unrelated creative scene merely because exact primary text is unavailable.

## Use larger context deliberately

A frontier model can attempt wider arc comparisons or multiple candidate structures when the task benefits. Allocate space to interacting consequences, counterevidence and the user's accepted prose before loading additional general reference material. More available context does not establish that the model has read, remembered or understood every loaded passage.

At a context handoff, retain the accepted decision, its reason, locator, unresolved question and next action. Keep summaries short enough to inspect. Preserve contradiction markers rather than smoothing them into a confident narrative. Reread the decisive source and latest accepted scene after a long interruption when either could change the next step.

If independent reviewers are available and their use is authorised, separate questions such as continuity, Hungarian naturalness and plot causality. Give each the same accepted brief and sufficient evidence, not the preferred verdict. Resolve disagreements against the text; majority agreement is not primary evidence.

## Stable contracts, adaptable effort

Keep language-neutral anchors, explicit evidence types, portable Markdown and dependency-free retrieval interfaces stable as model capabilities change. Re-evaluate actual behaviour when changing models or prompts. Treat longer context, tool choice and review depth as adjustable effort, not a fixed vendor-specific recipe or a promise about future models.

The architecture uses on-demand references as described in the [Agent Skills specification](https://agentskills.io/specification) and question-led retrieval consistent with [context engineering guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents). Actual loading and compaction depend on the host client.
