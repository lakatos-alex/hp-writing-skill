# Character arcs and relationships
Read alongside the short character map; this file supplies evidence anchors and scene-level decisions. References use book abbreviations defined in [source index](source-index.md). **Event** means a book event; **reading** is interpretation; **proposal** is a possible fanfic development, never a claimed canonical future.

## Harry: rescue, shame, and trust
**Event anchors:** PS16 distributes the final obstacles among all three friends. CoS18 connects Harry's identity to his choices. OotP23 follows his fear of possession and retreat into isolation; Ginny's experience challenges that isolation. OotP37 exposes the cost of Dumbledore withholding information. DH34 makes his final approach to death a deliberate choice.
**Reading:** Harry's urge to protect can become refusal to let other people decide what risks to take. The problem is not insufficient kindness but fear expressed through solitary action.
**Scene use:** give him a concrete danger to prioritise, then a person whose consent complicates his plan. He can be perceptive about suffering and wrong about another person's motives in the same scene.
**Dialogue:** typically direct under pressure; impatience and dry literal replies can coexist with uncertainty. Avoid making every line a speech about destiny.
**Adult proposal:** responsibility can turn rescue into delegation. Show the difficult moment when he trusts someone else with a task, rather than simply announcing he has matured.

## Hermione: knowledge, justice, and control
**Event anchors:** PS16 gives her a logic problem, while Ron handles chess. GoF9 and GoF21 expose house-elf exploitation and the different responses of Winky and Dobby. GoF28 shows Hermione reasoning about magical eavesdropping using Hogwarts' technological interference. DH15 supplies her explanation of food limitations.
**Reading:** she is willing to challenge accepted norms; her accuracy about injustice does not make every strategy effective. Preparedness is also how she manages fear.
**Scene use:** distinguish factual accuracy from interpersonal timing. She can identify the right problem and still need to listen to the person affected.
**Dialogue:** qualifying a claim, asking for evidence, correcting an assumption; allow irritation, mischief and affection. Technical speech should serve an urgent question, not recite an encyclopedia.
**Adult proposal:** a meeting with specialists should let her ask sharp questions without magically acquiring their years of experience.

## Ron: belonging, strategic competence, and repair
**Event anchors:** PS12 makes chess familiar family material; PS16 makes it necessary expertise. GoF17–20 covers the rupture around Harry's selection and its repair after the dragon. DH15 shows the departure under sustained hunger, fear and the locket's influence. DH19 dramatises return and confrontation with insecurity. DH31 makes concern for the elves part of his relationship with Hermione.
**Reading:** humour carries social awareness as well as avoidance. The locket intensifies a vulnerability; it does not invent all his responsibility or absolve him of it.
**Scene use:** give Ron information the others lack: wizarding habits, family consequences, an opponent's likely move. Allow the injured person time before accepting an apology.
**Dialogue:** practical questions, jokes rooted in the immediate predicament, blunt reactions when embarrassed. Do not reserve every sensible observation for Hermione.
**Adult proposal:** confidence can grow while comparison with wealth or fame still hurts.

## Ginny: being recognised as a participant
**Event anchors:** CoS17 reveals her exploitation through the diary. OotP23 lets her respond from lived experience when Harry fears possession. HBP24–25 develops the relationship with Harry; HBP30 supplies the separation at the funeral.
**Reading:** her confidence is not evidence that the diary ceased to matter. Her ability to challenge Harry is relational expertise, not supernatural emotional insight.
**Scene use:** she should have a task and an opinion before she becomes somebody's reassurance. Let her refuse the protective story another person tells about her.
**Dialogue:** concise, teasing, capable of a sudden serious correction. Avoid making sharpness her only mode.
**Adult proposal:** partnership negotiates whose risk counts, whose work is delayed, and who gets the last word in an emergency.

## Neville and Luna: two distinct outsiders
**Neville anchors:** PS16–17, standing against friends; OotP23, the closed ward and the wrapper he keeps; DH29, organised resistance and use of the Room; DH36, resistance to Voldemort and Nagini's death.
**Neville reading:** competence accumulates around convictions. The wrapper is valuable to him because of its relationship, regardless of its material value. His family history should not become gossip used solely to move another character.
**Luna anchors:** OotP10 introduces her; OotP38 connects her unusual perspective to loss; DH21 reveals the friends painted in her room and her father's vulnerability; DH25 shows her presence among survivors at Shell Cottage.
**Luna reading:** openness and perceptiveness coexist with unsupported beliefs. A character may question her explanation while respecting the experience behind it.
**Pairing caution:** outsider status does not make Neville and Luna interchangeable or automatically a canon couple.

## Dumbledore, McGonagall, and the ethics of authority
**Dumbledore anchors:** CoS18, choice; OotP37, explanation after loss; HBP27, negotiating with Draco; DH33 and DH35, plans, love, guilt and contested interpretation.
**Scene use:** separate what he knows, what he guesses, what he withholds, and what he rationalises. Other people can object coherently to a plan that has a protective goal.
**McGonagall anchors:** PS9, recognising Harry's flying and recruiting him; OotP29, career advice under Umbridge's interference; DH30–31, defence and evacuation.
**Scene use:** fairness and institutional loyalty need not mean passive compliance. She can offer protection through a precise instruction or an exception she takes responsibility for.
**Dialogue distinction:** Dumbledore may delay an answer through a question or image; McGonagall tends toward exact boundaries and actionable decisions. Neither should sound like a modern therapist explaining everyone to themselves.

## Snape, Lily, James, Sirius, Remus, Peter
**Evidence anchors:** PoA18–19 reconstruct the Marauders and betrayal; OotP28 shows James and Sirius bullying Snape, with Remus failing to intervene; DH33 reframes Snape's history with Lily and later work.
**Snape:** preserve espionage, remorse and cruelty simultaneously. The bravery of a secret mission does not remove a student's experience of humiliation.
**Lily:** her boundary with Snape has ethical content. She is not responsible for rehabilitating him or a prize transferred between men. Distinguish childhood friendship from consent to his later choices.
**James:** adolescent cruelty and later sacrifice both belong to the character. Do not claim the books provide a complete, scene-by-scene account of his reform.
**Sirius:** loyalty and resistance to his family coexist with recklessness and the influence of confinement. Let affection accidentally burden Harry with comparison to James.
**Remus:** PoA18 explains shame and dependence on Dumbledore's trust; DH11 shows how fear can turn into an offer to leave his pregnant wife. Gentleness is compatible with avoidance that others must confront.
**Peter:** betrayal is an action, not a personality aesthetic. PoA19 and DH23 offer evidence about self-preservation, past affiliation and a fatal moment of hesitation.
**Marauders-era caution:** knowledge comes from selected later testimony and memories. A complete school-year chronology, modern diagnostic labels, inherited aristocratic titles, and fandom-specific relationship assumptions require AU decisions.

## Draco and the Malfoy family
**Anchors:** CoS18, Lucius and Dobby; HBP2, Narcissa seeks protection through Snape; HBP27, Draco's task and hesitation; DH23, identification at the manor; DH36, Narcissa's lie.
**Draco:** fear under coercion does not undo earlier cruelty. Distinguish inability to kill at a particular moment from a completed ethical conversion.
**Narcissa:** family loyalty can oppose Voldemort without becoming egalitarian politics.
**Lucius:** status, violence and institutional influence matter before the family's fall. Losing influence changes tactics; it does not automatically change values.
**Redemption proposal:** show responsibility, reparative choices and resistance from people harmed. Do not require those people to forgive.

## Weasley relationships beyond Ron
**Arthur:** GoF7–9 puts curiosity beside Ministry work and response to violence. OotP7 shows professional marginality. OotP22–23 gives the family a tangible fear around his injury.
**Molly:** OotP5–9 combines hospitality, disputes over information, worry and a Boggart shaped by family loss. Care may become control, but care is still labour with real cost.
**Fred and George:** GoF37 supplies the shop funding; OotP29 turns spectacle into resistance; DH5 injures George, DH31 kills Fred. Never quietly substitute one twin's outcome for the other's.
**Percy:** ambition, family conflict and institutional allegiance culminate in return during DH30–31. Give reconciliation awkwardness and timing, not just a moral slogan.
**Bill and Fleur:** HBP29 tests others' assumptions about Fleur after Bill's injury; DH24–25 makes Shell Cottage a working refuge. Fleur's courage and competence deserve scenes beyond beauty or accent.
**Scene use:** large families should contain private alliances, interrupted conversations and unequal knowledge. A group meal is not a roll call in which everyone delivers one interchangeable joke.

## Supporting cast with useful independent motives
| Person | Book anchor | Writing use / distortion to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Dobby | CoS18; GoF21; DH23–24 | Freedom, paid work, chosen loyalty and costly action; not a generic servant or a tool summoned only for rescue. |
| Kreacher | OotP6, OotP37; DH10 | Inherited loyalty and mistreatment shape cooperation. A changed relationship requires evidence and practical treatment. |
| Winky | GoF8–9, GoF21, GoF35 | Loyalty, dismissal and distress; Dobby's wishes cannot stand for every elf's experience. |
| Hagrid | PS4–5; PoA6; GoF24 | Recognition of outsiders and flawed risk judgement; creature enthusiasm is not a reliable safety assessment. |
| Slughorn | HBP4, HBP17, HBP22–23 | Patronage, comfort, shame and selective memory; geniality can conceal consequential evasion. |
| Tonks | OotP3; HBP29; DH11 | Professional skill alongside awkwardness and emotional stakes; do not let romance erase her work. |
| Moody / false Moody | GoF14, GoF35; OotP3 | Fourth-year teaching is the impersonator's performance. Do not attribute every action to the real Auror. |
| Cedric | GoF20, GoF26, GoF31–32 | Competitive fairness and competence before victimhood; generosity does not make him narratively disposable. |
| Cho | OotP25–27 | Grief, conflicting loyalties and adolescent miscommunication; distinguish Cho from Marietta. |
| Umbridge | OotP13, OotP15, OotP32; DH13 | Violence administered as procedure and polite entitlement; effectiveness comes from institutional backing. |
| Fudge / Scrimgeour | GoF36; HBP16; DH7–8 | Denial, expediency and different political pressures; not interchangeable Ministries with one permanent policy. |
| Kingsley | OotP3; HBP1; DH31 | Discretion, cross-institution work and command; use actions rather than constant assertions of competence. |
| Ollivander / Griphook | PS5; DH24–26 | Expertise and different obligations; cooperation has terms and is not proof of shared values. |
