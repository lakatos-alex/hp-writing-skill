The controls below describe broad, reusable craft choices. All example prose is newly written for this guide and is not canon. The aim is an original project voice, not exact or near-exact imitation of a living author or translator. Do not extract distinctive formulations, preferred catchphrases or recurring sentence shapes from the books to assemble a substitute text.

## Information and rhythm

Hungarian word order can expose what is already shared and what is being corrected. Choose the intended contrast before polishing sound:

- `A kulcsot Júlia vitte el.` The key is under discussion; Júlia is identified as the person who took it.
- `Júlia a kulcsot vitte el, a levelet otthagyta.` The contrast concerns what she took.
- `Júlia elvitte a kulcsot.` A comparatively neutral report of the event.

Do not vary these only to avoid repeated words: they do different narrative work. Focus and negation also affect verbal prefixes: `Nem vitte el a kulcsot` is not repaired by restoring the prefix before the verb. Use Hungarian emphasis to place a correction or comic turn, rather than adding italics to every stressed word.

Drop personal pronouns when the verb and context identify the subject. Keep them for contrast or ambiguity: `Én őriztem az ajtót. Ő engedte be.` Repeat a name when two plausible actors could own an action. Hungarian's gender-neutral pronouns make clear action attribution particularly useful; do not replace every repeated name with `a fiú`, `a lány`, `az előbbi` and `az utóbbi`.

Translate temporal relationships, not English tense forms. Establish an earlier event with a clear cue, then narrate it without a stack of `már korábban` explanations:

> Előző este még nála volt a levél. Lefekvés előtt a tankönyvbe csúsztatta. Most a könyv nyitva hevert, a lapok között pedig nem volt semmi.

For a long paragraph, let a concrete observation lead to interpretation and then to a decision. Sentence length can follow attention rather than a fixed pattern of long description and short punchline:

> A lépcső alján megszakadtak a sáros nyomok. Dóra végignézett a száraz fokokon, aztán visszafordult. Aki itt járt, nem ment fel. A fülkében kellett keresnie.

Avoid filling every transition with `hirtelen`, `egyszerre`, `valahogy` or `mintha`. Keep them where they specify surprise, simultaneity or genuine uncertainty. When a comparison supplies no information beyond an adjective, try the direct observation first. An unusual image should grow from this viewpoint's experience and present need.

## Viewpoint and free indirect voice

Free indirect voice lets a third-person narrative carry a character's judgments without a thought tag or a switch to quoted first-person speech. Keep the knowledge boundary intact:

> A felügyelő a lezárt ajtóra mutatott. Persze, most már senki sem használhatta. Bezzeg tegnap, amikor Bence fél órán át állt odakint, még mindenki erre járt.

The complaint belongs to Bence. The narrator need not confirm that literally everyone used that door. An ensuing action can test the inference rather than restate the feeling:

> Bence megkerülte az asztalt. A kulcs helyén friss karcolást látott. Valaki már próbálkozott.

Distinguish three modes deliberately:

| Mode | Original example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Reported thought | `Dóra attól tartott, hogy elkésett.` | Names and summarizes the concern |
| Direct thought | `Elkéstem, gondolta Dóra.` | Presents her internal wording |
| Free indirect voice | `A folyosó üres volt. Hát persze. Már megint elkésett.` | Lets her interpretation enter the narration |

Do not classify every rhetorical question as free indirect narration: speaker, grammatical person and narrative frame matter. The sampled PoA11 opening moves between questions close to Harry's perception and explicitly attributed thought; OotP23 similarly contains both inferred fears and direct internal statements. Preserve the distinction rather than adopting an all-italic interior monologue template.

Close narration may name feelings. It may also report a mistaken explanation. Neither requires endorsing the character's judgment as a fact about another person's motive. If the viewpoint changes, establish that change; adding an omniscient quip in the middle of a close fear scene can expose information the character cannot possess.

## Dialogue as an attempt

Give each speaker an immediate objective and a limit on what they will reveal. Differentiate voices by precision, willingness to answer, preferred evidence, patience and social position before adding verbal habits. A character can speak differently to a friend, teacher and official while remaining recognizable.

> – Visszakaphatom a levelet?
>
> – Kinek vinnéd?
>
> – Nem ezt kérdeztem.
>
> – Tudom. Én viszont ezt kérdeztem.

The exchange has pressure because both speakers control information. Do not follow every line with an explanation of its subtext. If the next action changes the balance, give it room: the official might set the letter down within reach, or the pupil might stop reaching for it.

Hungarian particles such as `hát`, `csak`, `ugyan`, `már` and `azért` can change the interpersonal force of a line. Compare `Add ide` with `Add már ide` or `Csak add ide`. Choose the desired impatience, reassurance or restriction; indiscriminate particles make all speakers sound alike.

Use ordinary tags when identifying the speaker is enough. `Mondta` and `kérdezte` need not be replaced with a parade of louder verbs. An action beat is not a speech verb: someone can put down a cup before speaking, but cannot literally shrug a sentence. Avoid adding a gesture after every reply; repeated jaw-tightening and eyebrow movements soon become stage directions.

A British dialect does not require attaching a real Hungarian regional dialect to the character. Use broad differences in syntax, directness, formality and vocabulary unless the user's project establishes a researched dialect choice. Do not treat rural speech, foreignness or limited schooling as automatic stupidity. Broken orthography is not a shortcut to a voice.

## Humour, danger and institutional register

In the early-book samples, humour often comes from self-image colliding with observable conduct: PS1's insistence on normality, or CoS6's authority performance failing in practice. Transfer that relationship into a new situation, not the source's comic labels or image sequence:

> A prefektus szerint a sorban állás fegyelemre nevelt. Ezt már a sor elejéről mondta; oda a szabályzat kiosztása előtt beállt.

The joke exposes a small advantage. It does not require a sneering narrator after every sentence. In friendly dialogue, let the target answer, refuse the premise or win the next exchange. An endless supply of flawless retorts erases personality and consequence.

Early wonder can arise from a practical question: price, access, ownership, how to use something without embarrassment. PS6's food purchase matters to a new friendship as well as to discovery of the world. In a new scene, an ordinary lunch or a borrowed pen may do that work. An enchanted object earns attention by changing access, danger, evidence or a relationship; it does not need to wink, sigh or glow to establish the setting.

In danger, narrow attention to what can be perceived and acted on. Avoid importing the source's sensory signature. Invent a specific obstacle and a next choice:

> A kilincs elfordult a túloldalon. Márton levette a kezét a reteszről. Ha most hátralép, a padló megnyikordul; ha marad, az ajtó rányílik. A fal mellé húzódott, és intett a húgának, hogy ne mozduljon.

This creates fear through constrained movement. A momentary joke can show coping, but cannot erase a wound, pursuit or broken trust. Late-book danger also includes institutions deciding what counts as evidence. CoS14 already contains serious bureaucratic harm; the tonal development is not a clean division into funny early books and grim later books.

For an institutional voice, identify the procedural action: accuse, record, defer, exclude, authorize. Formal nouns, impersonal constructions and narrow questions can shift responsibility. Use them where the institution acts, while the surrounding narration remains readable:

> – A kérelmet határidőn túl nyújtotta be.
>
> – Tegnap még nem tudtam, hogy kell hozzá engedély.
>
> – A határidőt ez nem módosítja.

The harm is the blocked request, not the number of impressive office titles. Preserve real distinctions between a charge, testimony, judgment and administrative precaution. OotP8's question sequence controls Harry's opportunity to explain; HBP1's official concerns coexist with wider danger. Neither supplies a template for every ministry employee's personality.

## Emotion and practical attention

Restraint concerns how and when emotion becomes visible, not an obligation to avoid explicit feeling. In GoF36, comfort meets Harry's difficulty speaking about loss; in the OotP23 hospital scene, an observed small action carries a private attachment. Build new material from the present relationship, without recycling those props or situations:

> – Maradjak?
>
> Eszter a harmadik csészét is elmosta, pedig senki sem ivott belőle.
>
> – A kabátodat még ne vedd fel.

The answer requests company without a speech explaining the relationship. A later line may still name grief or loneliness if that is what this person would do. Do not append an abstract moral after the physical action has already made the change legible.

Care should affect workload, risk or choice. Someone checks a route, admits uncertainty, asks before helping, or takes over an agreed task. HBP26's exit has a concrete change in who supports whom; borrow the general mechanism of responsibility changing under pressure, not its closing formulation or staging. Reassurance should fit the evidence available to the speaker and may be fallible.
