# Deathly Hallows: custody, trust and choice

## Narrative orientation

DH1–8 stages preparation, flight, family life and institutional collapse. DH9–15 makes concealment, supplies and trust increasingly difficult. DH16–22 interleaves dangerous history, competing interpretations and the Hallows temptation. DH23–30 changes resources, alliances and access. DH31–36 brings battle, revelation and chosen action; the epilogue gives a limited future view.

## Practical pressures

Food, safe shelter, injuries, disguises and information routes affect decisions throughout the journey. DH15's food explanation and DH29's tunnel solution distinguish an abstract magical capability from logistics.

Ron’s departure and return have material and emotional context. The locket intensifies existing vulnerabilities; it does not absolve responsibility or create every feeling from nothing. Recovery of cooperation requires action.

## Objects and allegiance

Track the sword, wands, locket, cloak, book, Snitch and mirror through custody and knowledge. Possessing an object is not identical to understanding it or holding its allegiance. DH24 explicitly qualifies wand transfer by the object and manner of taking.

The bargain with Griphook requires attention to incompatible ownership assumptions. DH25's Bill explanation should retain its status as testimony from someone with experience. Avoid flattening the conflict into one irrational species refusing obvious terms.

## History with witnesses

Godric's Hollow, Rita's account, Aberforth and Snape's memories provide different routes into the past. Each has access, motives and limitations. DH33 reframes earlier events without erasing every injury or responsibility.

DH35's explanation occurs in an unusual encounter whose narrative status needs care. It should not become a universal formula for immortality. The final confrontation combines several particular mechanisms and choices.

## World details worth retrieving

- DH4, DH6–7: Trace, secrecy, adulthood and preparation.
- DH10–11: Kreacher, Regulus and Lupin's offer.
- DH15: food and group fracture.
- DH19: return, the sword and confrontation with the locket.
- DH22–26: Taboo, capture, rescue, wandlore and ownership.
- DH28–29: Aberforth, resistance and access.
- DH31–36: dangerous magic, loss, revelation and final choices.
- DH epilogue: family and school outcomes explicitly shown.

## Craft transfer

Let practical strain expose incompatible priorities. A shortage is most useful when it changes work, patience, risk or trust. A refuge can be a place for mourning and difficult decisions, not a pause in causality.

Competing quests need an opportunity cost. If a protagonist becomes fascinated with one explanation or artifact, show what work is delayed and who challenges the diversion.

A culminating sacrifice must belong to the person making it. Other people's plans, omissions and hopes can surround the choice without erasing its emotional reality.

## Continuation questions

The epilogue is a small window, not a complete post-war history. Neville's teaching role is shown; detailed appointment dates, reforms and every adult career are not. Use the post-war reference to develop open space explicitly.
