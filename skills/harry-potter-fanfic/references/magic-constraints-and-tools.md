# Magic: rules, limits, and useful uncertainties
Book citations are explained in [source index](source-index.md). This is a scene-planning reference, not a complete spell dictionary.

## Rules to establish before designing a magical solution
Record caster knowledge, tool, target, conditions, time, failure mode and who can observe the attempt. Canon does not supply a universal numerical mana system, magical-core scale, rune programming language or formal taxonomy of wards. Those can be AU inventions, but cannot silently justify every new capability.

| Mechanism | Book evidence | Consequence for a scene |
|---|---|---|
| Wand choice and allegiance | PS5; DH24; DH36 | Compatibility, conquest and allegiance matter, but every disarming is not a mechanically identical ownership transfer. Ollivander admits complexity. |
| Patronus | PoA12; PoA21; OotP18 | Difficult defensive magic with emotional demands; success in practice is not guaranteed under real Dementor pressure. |
| Boggart | PoA7; OotP9 | Fear becomes embodied. A revealing image is not a comprehensive diagnosis of its subject. |
| Animagus | PoA18–19 | Learned, difficult transformation, registry and unregistered cases. Distinguish it from involuntary lycanthropy. |
| Wolfsbane | PoA18 | Preserves the mind during transformation when used as described; it does not cure lycanthropy. |
| Polyjuice | CoS12; GoF35; DH4, DH26 | Preparation, identity access and practical mistakes create risk. Do not assume every disguise gives memories, skills or legal authority. |
| Occlumency / Legilimency | OotP24; HBP28 | Intrusion and defence are difficult and relationally fraught. Avoid treating Legilimency as an infallible transcript of another mind. |
| Unbreakable Vow | HBP2; HBP16 | Binding promises have lethal stakes. Do not generalise all ordinary spoken promises into magical contracts. |
| Fidelius | PoA10; DH6, DH14 | Specify the secret, keeper, disclosure chain and changes after a keeper's death. It is not a universal invisibility toggle. |
| Underage Trace | CoS2; DH4, DH9, DH11 | Nearby magic and the caster's identity are not equivalent information. Do not make it a permanent adult tracking implant. |
| Food | DH15; DH29 | Food cannot simply be created from nothing; the Room supplies access to food through Aberforth. Kitchens, stocks and deliveries matter. |
| Enchanted communication | OotP19; HBP27 | Hermione's coins and Draco's adaptation show ingenuity and unintended reuse. Establish who holds the receiving object. |
| Dangerous fire | DH31 | Fiendfyre can destroy a Horcrux but is disastrously difficult to control. A powerful solution may destroy the evidence, place and people too. |
| Healing | CoS10; OotP23; HBP29 | Some injuries mend rapidly; others remain. Emotional and cognitive harm do not disappear because the hospital has magic. |
| Horcruxes | HBP23; DH6; DH31 | Soul fragmentation, preparation and destructive conditions matter. Do not conflate every cursed object with a Horcrux. |

## Travel and access
**Apparition:** HBP18 establishes lessons; DH14 demonstrates injury during escape. Hogwarts normally restricts Apparition; temporary teaching exceptions and house-elf movement prevent a blanket rule that every magical being obeys identical restrictions. Set destination knowledge, security, companions and injury risk.
**Floo:** CoS4 turns travel into an embarrassing navigational failure; OotP29 and OotP32 show why fireplaces are also communication and surveillance surfaces. A hearth is not automatically a registered, available network terminal.
**Portkeys:** GoF6 establishes scheduled collective transport; GoF32 makes destination substitution dangerous. Ask who arranged it, when it activates and who is touching it.
**Brooms, carriages, boats and trains:** retaining material journeys gives time for relationships, fatigue and exposure. Do not use Apparition to erase school rituals or every rural travel problem.
**House-elves:** DH23 shows escape from captivity using different capabilities. Treat those capabilities as belonging to a person with motives, not an emergency loophole the protagonist owns.

## Information tools are imperfect
The Marauder's Map helps identify people and paths (PoA10, PoA17–18); it does not answer every question about every space. Pensieve memories supply access to the past, but HBP17 explicitly introduces altered memory. Portraits, ghosts and the Resurrection Stone are different phenomena; none should be flattened into unlimited consultation with a resurrected original person.

**Prophecy:** OotP37 and HBP23 emphasise choice and Voldemort's response. Prediction should generate interpretation and decisions, not remove agency.
**Truth:** confessions, Veritaserum and memories require source and circumstance. An author should not assume the Ministry can solve all prosecutions through one potion without political, evidentiary or practical obstacles.

## Hogwarts and electronics
GoF28 has Hermione explain that electricity, computers and radar malfunction around Hogwarts because of its magical environment. This is a concrete obstacle, not a claim that every Muggle-made mechanical object fails everywhere. Distinguish electrical operation, mechanical operation and a new magical analogue. A network, radio or computer project needs an explicit workaround and limits, with the workaround labelled as the fanfic's invention.
## Spell selection without incantation clutter
Choose the effect required by the action, then verify the incantation if the spelling matters. Use ordinary learned spells for ordinary tasks. Reserve named rare magic for a problem that needs its distinctive rule. Do not add a shouted incantation to every movement simply to make a paragraph feel magical.
