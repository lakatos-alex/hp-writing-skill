# Wizarding-world foundations

Use this reference for setting choices and social dynamics. It is a compact consistency guide, not a replacement for checking a plot-critical detail in the books.

## Places as dramatic systems

| Place | What it enables | Useful pressure |
|---|---|---|
| Hogwarts | Education, hierarchy, ritual, house identity, secret architecture, and adult oversight that is never complete | School rules, house loyalties, staff authority, student knowledge gaps, and a building with history. |
| The Great Hall and common rooms | Public performance versus private belonging | Rumour, social sorting, food and celebration, allegiance, exclusion, and overheard information. |
| Hogsmeade | A supervised threshold between school and wider society | First independence, commerce, secrets, unsupervised meetings, and consequences returning to school. |
| Diagon Alley and Knockturn Alley | The magical economy and its moral gradients | Consumer desire, class difference, legitimate versus dubious trade, information markets, and danger close to ordinary life. |
| The Ministry of Magic | Bureaucracy, law, propaganda, public legitimacy, and institutional memory | Delays, compromised authority, competing departments, political pressure, and the gap between procedure and justice. |
| The Burrow, Grimmauld Place, and other homes | Family inheritance and chosen safety | Hospitality, old conflict, grief, secrecy, and the difference between shelter and control. |
| St Mungo's and Azkaban | The limits and moral cost of magical institutions | Care, trauma, stigma, punishment, memory, and what magic cannot easily repair. |

## World dynamics

### Magic has capability and limits

Magic changes logistics, but it should not erase stakes. Ask what knowledge, tool, training, consent, time, cost, risk, or legal constraint a spell requires. Death, trauma, prejudice, grief, trust, and institutional damage are not cleanly solved by a wand.

### Secrecy is social, not only technical

The Statute of Secrecy shapes travel, careers, family stories, Muggle contact, law enforcement, and who gets believed. A secret can be protected by charms, but also by fear, loyalty, bureaucracy, social rank, or a person's choice to stay silent.

### Blood prejudice is a power system

Pure-blood ideology is political and material: it allocates legitimacy, fear, family expectation, and access. Do not treat blood status as destiny or turn bigotry into decorative villain shorthand. Show how institutions, households, peers, and resistance reproduce or challenge it.

### Institutions contain contradiction

Hogwarts, the Ministry, families, and resistance groups can shelter people and fail them at the same time. Adults may be brave yet compromised; rules may prevent harm or preserve injustice. Let characters make choices inside those tensions.

### Community carries the plot

Owls, Floo travel, Patronuses, Apparition, portraits, newspapers, shops, and informal family networks make information move unevenly. Treat communication and travel as story engines: who can reach whom, who is delayed, what is overheard, and what a method costs.

### Economy and status matter

Money, inheritance, class, school reputation, Ministry access, and family name shape options without entirely determining character. Practical details—books, travel, work, housing, care—can show these pressures more credibly than exposition.

## Local canon rule

An AU, post-war continuation, or adult story may alter any element above. State the divergence, its consequence, and who knows it. Once established, honour it consistently.
