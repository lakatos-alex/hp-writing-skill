# Character and relationship workshop

Use for character development, ensemble scenes, believable dialogue, romance, antagonists and redemption. Canon provides actions and selected access to inner lives; psychological readings remain interpretations.

## Character at this moment

Begin with the story's era and accepted divergences. Identify:
- Present want and what the person risks losing.
- Enduring value and the strategy they use to protect it.
- Competence useful in this scene.
- Blind spot likely to create friction.
- Current knowledge and mistaken belief.
- A boundary they can defend independently of the protagonist.

Avoid assigning a permanent diagnosis from a single scene. Different circumstances can expose courage, avoidance, tenderness and cruelty in one person. Read the short character map for orientation, then the relationship reference and decisive book passages.

## Relationship as two perspectives

For each side, distinguish trust in competence, trust in honesty, comfort with vulnerability and willingness to accept help. These can change at different rates. A character may trust someone in a fight while withholding a family secret.

Make care actionable. Who notices fatigue, does unpleasant work, remembers a constraint, or asks before acting? Also identify what care costs and when it becomes control. An argument can be about the method of protection rather than lack of affection.

## Dialogue differentiation

Give each speaker a tactic: ask for specifics, deflect with humour, appeal to rules, expose a contradiction, make a practical offer, or refuse to answer. Tactics change with audience and pressure. Canon does not justify making every Hermione line a correction or every Ron line a joke.

Read aloud for conversational cause and effect. A response should react to what was actually said, including evasion and emotional stakes. Don't require all characters to speak in polished summaries of their own motivations.

## Ensemble scenes

Distribute expertise and agendas. Let someone be occupied with a different problem, arrive with partial information, or leave before consensus. A shared meal works through alliances and interruptions rather than one equal line per person. Track who hears each disclosure.

## Growth, grief and repair

Show growth as a changed choice under comparable pressure, supported by experience. A return after failure should cost something; an apology does not require immediate forgiveness. Redemption concerns responsibility and sustained action, including how the harmed person responds.

A later revelation about Snape does not erase a student's humiliation. Draco's inability to kill Dumbledore at one moment does not establish completed reform. Narcissa's protection of her son does not prove egalitarian politics. Keep ethical distinctions specific.

For grief, vary practical functioning and emotional response. A capable person can have a bad day without resetting their whole arc. Do not use another person's suffering only to deepen the protagonist.

Deliver a character brief, scene revision or relationship arc at the requested scope. Keep invented backstory and future development labelled as proposals until accepted.
