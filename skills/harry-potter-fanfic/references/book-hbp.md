# Half-Blood Prince: preparation and incomplete memory

## Narrative orientation

HBP1–6 presents war through political contact, family pressure and suspicious activity. HBP7–17 interleaves school life, inherited expertise and Dumbledore's selected historical evidence. HBP18–25 develops investigation, persuasion and relationship changes. HBP26–30 brings a costly expedition, a confrontation, loss and a decision about the next quest.

## Two kinds of inherited knowledge

The annotated textbook offers immediately useful techniques while hiding the identity and ethics of its author. Pensieve lessons offer historical evidence whose selection and reliability need interpretation. Neither source is simply “knowledge that solves the problem.”

HBP17 makes altered recollection explicit. HBP22–23 changes access to the missing account. A research scene becomes dramatic when gaining evidence depends on trust, shame, persuasion or a decision about how to treat the witness.

## Parallel secret projects

Harry investigates while Draco works under coercive pressure. Their unequal information creates both real suspicion and misinterpretation. Adults have separate commitments and limits. Dumbledore's immobilisation of Harry on the tower in HBP27 has consequences for action and later interpretation.

Narcissa's request in HBP2 establishes maternal protection through a particular ritual; Ron's explanation in HBP16 supplies its lethal stakes. Keep the Vow separate from ordinary promises and the Tournament contract.

## Relationships under strain

Romantic and social changes need their own causes rather than serving only as respite between investigation scenes. Fleur's response to Bill's injury in HBP29 challenges others' assumptions about her. Do not reduce her contribution to appearance or a foreign accent.

Draco's hesitation under threat is evidence of conflict, not proof that all previous prejudice was a mask. A later redemption arc must develop from choices beyond this moment.

## World details worth retrieving

- HBP2: the Vow and its participants.
- HBP4: Slughorn's recruitment and patronage.
- HBP9: the potions classroom and Felix Felicis.
- HBP17: edited memory.
- HBP18: controlled Apparition instruction.
- HBP22–23: access to Slughorn's true account and Horcrux discussion.
- HBP27: disarming, constrained movement and the tower.
- HBP29–30: injury, grief, funeral and separation.

## Craft transfer

Make research selective: why is this memory shown now, who controls it, what does it explain, and what does it omit? An archive scene should change a working theory or a relationship with the source.

A technical shortcut can improve performance while increasing dependence and risk. Show the attraction before its cost. Avoid condemning curiosity itself when the actual failure is using a tool without understanding what it does.

## Continuation questions

What has Harry learned about Horcruxes, what has he told others, who knows Dumbledore's condition or agreement with Snape, and who understands the wand consequences? Later revelations do not retroactively become common knowledge.
