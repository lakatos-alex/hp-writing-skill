# A small continuity check for a living scene

Use when a scene depends on a handoff, a secret, constrained movement, an injury or a promise. For a simple exchange, a mental check is enough. Use the host's existing notes; no new ledger or visible planning block is required.

## Before and after, not every motion

Identify only the state that changes a character's options:

| Question | Useful distinction |
|---|---|
| Who has it? | Ownership, current custody and permission to use are different. An offer is not a completed handoff. |
| Where can they act? | Position, reach, line of sight and who can overhear matter at the decisive action, not at every step. |
| Who knows it? | Observation, testimony, suspicion and reader-only knowledge remain separate. |
| What limits them? | Injury, skill, time, tools and obligations survive convenient transitions. |
| What did they agree? | A proposal, acceptance, refusal and promise are different states. |

For example, an invented scene might begin: `Sára holds the damaged compass; Imre knows the return deadline; neither has agreed to carry it.` The scene need not state that note. Its actions should let a reader reconstruct the consequential changes.

## Check the written scene

Trace each important change to an action or information route. Someone reaches out; that alone does not tell us whether the other person lets go. Someone says the repair is safe; the viewpoint character may believe them without the narrator certifying it. If an earlier limitation no longer matters, show what changed it.

At the moment of consequence, give enough spatial or grammatical detail to disambiguate the action. Then return attention to the character's purpose. Repeating every person's name and every object's location can make clear prose lifeless. Clarify the hinge, not the entire paragraph.

Before calling something a contradiction, state the two incompatible facts and check the actual wording around both. A pronoun with two possible referents is a clarity finding unless context establishes the impossible reading. In Hungarian, an omitted subject or `felé` may require a fuller sentence to resolve; see [terminology and reference](hungarian-terminology.md#check-who-gives-receives-and-owns).

## Turn continuity into narrative pressure

Preserving state is necessary, but not enough for a good scene. Ask how it changes the next choice. A borrowed object can prevent someone leaving; a withheld fact can change a negotiation; a promise can cost the speaker time. Let participants pursue different reasonable outcomes. Do not use the check to force agreement, punish refusal or resolve every tension.

If a change is unclear, prefer a small repair at the relevant beat: an acceptance, a named recipient, the message that carried the news, or an acknowledged remaining limitation. In a prose-only edit, do not invent new events to repair missing setup without approval for developmental changes.

For complex serial work, continue with [continuity and AU](workflow-continuity.md). The optional `check-state` tool checks supplied structured events, not everything implied by a manuscript; passing it does not certify the prose.
