# Prisoner of Azkaban: history revised

## Narrative orientation

PoA1–6 brings escape, apparent pursuit, Dementors and a new teacher. PoA7–16 develops fear, restricted access, practical training and danger interpreted through an accepted history. PoA17–19 forces competing witnesses and explanations together. PoA20–22 tests what the protagonists can do with revised knowledge and a constrained second pass through events.

## Knowledge layers

PoA10 explains Fidelius while the adults assume the wrong betrayal story. A valid rule can be embedded in false historical interpretation. Read claims individually rather than assigning an entire conversation a single truth value.

The Map helps identify people and routes but does not explain motives. Animagus knowledge changes the meaning of earlier appearances. Recognition, proof and institutional acceptance remain different problems: learning Sirius's innocence does not instantly exonerate him publicly.

The time sequence demands attention to what was observed, what was assumed and what becomes understood later. Use the book's actual events for its specific rescue; do not generalise to every possible time-travel model.

## Fear and competence

Lupin teaches through practical support and graded difficulty. PoA7's Boggart work involves mental framing and group dynamics. PoA12's Patronus lessons distinguish explanation from repeated performance. PoA21 gives confidence a basis in action and recognition.

Lupin's teaching skill coexists with shame, dependence on others' acceptance and serious mistakes. Sirius's innocence of one accusation does not remove every aspect of recklessness or cruelty elsewhere in his history. Peter's survival strategies should be assessed as choices, not a visual shorthand.

## World details worth retrieving

- PoA3: the Knight Bus as another form of travel.
- PoA5: Dementor effects, recovery and school security.
- PoA7: Boggart teaching and the role of laughter.
- PoA8–10: Hogsmeade access, permission and the Map.
- PoA12: Patronus instruction.
- PoA18–19: Animagi, the registry, Wolfsbane and betrayal history.
- PoA21–22: rescue timing, perception and aftermath.

## Craft transfer

Bring conflicting accounts into a scene where each speaker has something to lose. Let the audience revise an explanation while physical danger remains. A reveal need not stop the action.

Teach a capability before demanding its most difficult use. Early failure should reveal the specific obstacle—concentration, fear, knowledge or timing—so later success feels earned.

For time travel, maintain an event table that separates experienced order from chronological order. Decide whether the fanfic uses a consistent loop or an explicit AU with changeable history. The mechanism should remain stable across rescue and consequence.

## Continuation questions

Who knows Sirius is innocent? Who knows Peter's form? Who has access to the Map? What can the Ministry prove? Which relationship changes because Harry has a godfather even if living arrangements remain constrained? These distinctions prevent a revelation from becoming universal knowledge.
