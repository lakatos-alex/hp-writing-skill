# Philosopher's Stone: discovery and belonging

## Narrative orientation

PS1–4 contrasts public magical celebration with Harry's constrained domestic understanding. PS5–7 turns world entry into transactions, travel and sorting. PS8–13 lets ordinary lessons, sport, rule-breaking and friendship accumulate information. PS14–17 brings practical consequences and the final confrontation together. These ranges are orientation, not a claim that every chapter performs only one function.

## Knowledge architecture

Harry's ignorance makes explanation motivated when he needs to buy supplies, reach school or survive a lesson. A helpful guide can still be partial or biased. Hagrid introduces a world through affection and expertise while also mishandling dangerous secrets. Snape's conspicuous hostility is not identical to responsibility for the central plot.

The Mirror supplies a useful distinction between emotionally compelling information and truth: Dumbledore explicitly rejects treating it as knowledge in PS12. A fanfic can use an object that reveals a longing without making it a diagnostic instrument or promise of a future outcome.

## Characters and relationships

PS10 makes shared danger and an unexpected protective account change the trio's relationship. PS16 distributes necessary competencies: physical action, chess, observation and logic matter at different moments. Avoid turning later expertise into a reason one person could always have solved everything.

Harry's entry into community does not instantly remove insecurity formed at home. Ron's familiarity with wizarding life gives him information Harry lacks; his material circumstances affect comparison and embarrassment. Hermione's knowledge has social friction before it becomes shared strength.

## World details worth retrieving

- PS5: school purchasing, banking, coin ratios and wand fitting.
- PS6–7: platform access, first arrival, houses and public ceremony.
- PS8–10: teachers, lessons, flying and rules.
- PS12–13: holidays, the library, the Mirror and identification of Flamel.
- PS15–17: punishment, dangerous access and the final obstacles.

Use the full chapter for exact phrasing, who is present and what is learned. Do not infer a complete curriculum, economy or legal system from a shopping trip and selected lessons.

## Craft transfer

A setting feels large when a practical task exposes another system. Introduce an unfamiliar place through a purchase, application, arrival or mistake. Let the newcomer discover that one person's confidence is not universal authority.

Belonging becomes credible through reciprocal action. Somebody needs help, gives information, shares something scarce, or takes a risk. Keep the emotional change connected to what happened rather than declaring the group inseparable immediately.

The ending's explanation should account for earlier evidence and leave consequences. Do not simply remove the suspicious person and call the mystery solved.

## Continuation questions

At an early-school cutoff, who knows about the Stone, the Mirror's mechanism, the cloak's source and the protection around Harry? These are different disclosures. Later Horcrux knowledge cannot appear in first-year dialogue without a route or AU.

Interpretive claims here are craft analysis. The precise mechanism of Lily's protection needs PS17 and later books together.
