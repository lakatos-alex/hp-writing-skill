# Scene craft and revision
These are original writing techniques inferred from book scenes, not instructions to imitate an author's sentences.

## Give the setting a job
PS5 introduces a world through purchases and decisions. CoS3 shows family through a home. GoF7–9 lets a public celebration expose international scale and political danger.
**Apply:** a scene needs an activity with friction. Let one practical detail force a choice. Avoid a paragraph of famous objects that none of the characters needs.

## Distribute expertise
PS16 uses different competencies for different obstacles. A cast becomes believable when the solution would change if any participant were absent.
**Apply:** assign each participant a contribution, mistaken assumption and stake. The smart character need not answer everything; the funny character may recognise the decisive practical detail.

## Build a mystery around wrong explanations
CoS13–17 and PoA17–19 change the interpretation of prior evidence. A reveal works when facts become newly meaningful, not when an unmentioned power is invented.
**Apply:** record clue, surface interpretation, true implication, who has seen it, and when the interpretation changes. An ominous object must affect somebody's behaviour before the explanation.
**Revision question:** could the reader look back and understand the error without the narration having lied?

## Let an object carry a relationship
OotP23's sweet wrapper is moving because of its giver and keeper, not its market value.
**Apply:** choose a detail that belongs to these people. Later recurrence should change its emotional meaning. Do not explain the symbol immediately after a character's action already makes it clear.

## Use aftermath as plot
GoF36–37 turns an ordeal into testimony, denial, grief and decisions. OotP37 makes explanation itself a conflict.
**Apply:** after danger, decide who believes the account, who must clean up, who feels excluded, and what responsibility changes hands. Recovery can progress the story without a new attack.

## Social scenes contain incompatible agendas
A ball, lesson or shared meal need not become filler. GoF23 places appearance, jealousy and political/social encounter in one public setting.
**Apply:** define private wants and public constraints. Some characters can talk around the issue; others can misread the silence. Give scene partners permission to leave with different understandings.

## Dialogue and point of view
Choose the focal character before drafting. Filter sensory details through what matters to them now. Distinguish an inference from direct access to another person's thoughts.
When reviewing dialogue, remove names temporarily: can motives and response patterns distinguish speakers? Restore names where clarity needs them. Avoid a caricature in which every speaker has only one verbal tic.
Subtext needs a legible surface interaction: passing a book, requesting a favour, refusing an invitation. Characters should not speak only in elegant evasions.

## Long chapters
A requested long chapter needs multiple changes, not more adjectives. Allocate space to opening pressure, development, complication, decision and aftermath; adjust to the genre and user request. Record the actual word count with the counting method. Do not report a target as achieved without measuring the saved artifact.

## Romance and character agency
Give both participants an independent want and something they can decline. The scene may show awkwardness, teasing, reassurance or repair; intensity is not a substitute for specificity.
A partner's improved confidence should appear in self-directed choices beyond the relationship. Do not convert vulnerability into perpetual dependence.
Follow the requested age-appropriate scope and rating. Keep affection and emotional consequence present when a scene fades.

## Revision passes
1. **Causality:** what changes, and which choice caused it?
2. **Character:** did anyone become conveniently foolish or all-knowing?
3. **World:** what rule is established, inferred or invented?
4. **Continuity:** time, location, knowledge, injuries, objects, promises.
5. **Prose:** repeated intensifiers, generic luxury, repeated body descriptions, identical sentence openings, redundant explanations.
6. **Handoff:** what changed in the story state, and what remains unresolved?

Record problems with specific passages and effects. A word count or automated search can identify repetition; it cannot certify literary quality.

## Evidence-led continuity revision

Before a consequential edit, identify the intended change, what must remain true, and the affected passages. Read the target, its adjacent scenes, and relevant established records; search earlier setup and later payoff only where the change reaches them.

Track a small before/after state in the existing notes: time and location; possession and condition of important objects; physical limitations; relationship agreements; outstanding promises. For a secret, distinguish knowing, suspecting, believing incorrectly, and what the reader alone knows. Require a scene, message, observation or justified inference for each transfer of knowledge. A later revelation cannot retroactively inform earlier dialogue.

Trace consequences through decisions: if the new fact changes a character's options, check the next dependent action and any payoff. An intentional unanswered question is not a continuity error. A recurring motif should develop or serve a recognizable purpose; do not mechanically remove it as repetition.

For findings, give severity, passage location, conflicting evidence, and the smallest repair; separate contradictions from taste suggestions. When authorized to edit, update affected existing records from the saved result, not from an abandoned outline. Keep proposals, alternatives and unresolved uncertainty distinguishable from accepted events. Use the host's established formatting and validation tools; never rewrite prose simply to silence a mechanical warning.

## Optional voice-preserving prose pass

Calibrate against a few representative, author-approved passages from this project: narration, dialogue and an emotionally important scene. AI-assisted origins do not disqualify prose the author has chosen. Note POV and tense, paragraph rhythm, degree of interiority, humour, imagery, speaker differences and forms of address. These observations guide judgment; they are not numerical quotas.

Inspect repeated constructions in context: interchangeable reactions, repeated contrasts, explanations after a gesture has already conveyed the emotion, generic declarations of significance, and identical paragraph endings. Change a passage only when its effect is identifiable. Preserve deliberate refrains, colloquial language, ornate passages, ambiguity and pauses that belong to this story. No blacklist, authorship detector, punctuation purge, or compulsory plainness.

Apply the smallest effective edit. A prose-only pass preserves events, clues, motives, promises, uncertainty and relationship boundaries. Do not invent a joke, memory, sensory event or new magical capability to make the revision more vivid; place any needed development in the existing editorial notes. This restriction governs revision, not authorized new-story invention.

Compare the edited scene with its original and the calibration passages. Has a character lost their particular way of speaking? Has subtext become explanation, uncertainty become certainty, or intimacy become generic reassurance? Restore anything the edit flattened. Read dialogue for speakability, retaining the project's punctuation and paragraph rules. Recheck required length with the existing counting method; do not pad or silently shorten the agreed chapter target.

### Magical and literary specificity

Personification may describe an actual magical agent, a viewpoint impression or a metaphor. Establish which before editing it away or treating it as a new power. A repeated magical detail may be a clue. Preserve its information even when revising its wording; do not replace distinctive wizarding texture with generic domestic prose. The accepted fanwork supplies the prose baseline, while the books supply world evidence.
