# Objects, custody and emotional consequence

Use when a story relies on an artifact, a clue, an inheritance, a gift or a recurring domestic object.

## Four independent fields

Track physical holder, condition, access to use and understood meaning. Magical allegiance may add a fifth field. A wand can be held without choosing its user. A sword can be available without the group understanding why it matters. A gift can mean different things to giver and recipient.

## Planting and payoff

Introduce an object through use, not only description. A broken clasp may frustrate a task before becoming relevant; a borrowed book can establish trust before a hidden note changes its meaning. Preserve the object's earlier information during revision.

A payoff may be practical, emotional or interpretive. It does not always require activating a new power. Returning an ordinary object can demonstrate repair in a relationship.

## Gifts, inheritance and obligation

Ask whether the recipient can decline, whether they understand the object's history, and what the giver expects. A valuable gift can create dependence or social pressure. A mundane gift can acknowledge a private need.

Do not infer hereditary titles, magical heir tests or absolute legal ownership from the existence of an inheritance. A story can invent these systems explicitly, with terms and consequences.

## Artifact research

Separate manufacture, reputation, demonstrated function and current condition. A legend about an artifact is not the same as observed performance. Keep who knows the research result distinct from who currently holds the object.

When designing an original artifact, set its one or two meaningful capabilities before adding a history. Too many convenient functions weaken choices. Give failures a coherent cause rather than arbitrary refusal.

## Revision checks

For every consequential transfer, locate the handing over, theft, loss, storage or discovery. If custody changes offstage, establish a credible route. A damaged wand does not become repaired between chapters without an event or accepted time jump. An object retrieved by one viewpoint character is not automatically available to another.
