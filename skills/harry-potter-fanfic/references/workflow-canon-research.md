# Canon research

Use for a factual question, disputed rule, cross-book pattern or research brief. Output a supported answer with the relevant uncertainty, not a dump of search results.

## Establish the question

Separate the proposition from its implications. “Can the Room feed fugitives?” contains at least three questions: whether it creates food, whether it supplies access to food, and what Neville knew about it. DH29 answers these differently. Define the date and people affected before searching.

Use the chapter catalogue to locate candidates, then read the passage around the match. Search terms are hypotheses, not proof. A name can appear in a rumour, a memory, a joke or a chapter that disproves the initial assumption. Search variant spellings, aliases and plural forms if the literal query misses.

## Work at the needed depth

- A local check usually needs a passage and adjacent reactions.
- A rules question may need the first explanation, an actual use and an exception.
- A character argument may need an early belief, a costly choice, and later consequences.
- A full-corpus assignment may require a book-by-book reading plan and a coverage record. Use the complete sources; record chapters actually read and unresolved leads. Machine scans alone are structural coverage.

Read beyond an isolated match until the speaker, referent, relevant action and qualification are clear. Return to an earlier source if a later revelation changes its meaning. PoA10 gives a valid description of Fidelius inside a conversation whose assumed betrayer is wrong; the source is not uniformly reliable or uniformly useless.

## Build the answer

For each conclusion, retain a compact evidence chain:
1. Claim in your own words.
2. Book and chapter, with the scene or speaker.
3. Evidence type: event, testimony or interpretation.
4. What the passage does not establish.
5. Consequence for the user's scene.

Compare witnesses without flattening them into a consensus. Bill's explanation of goblin ownership in DH25 is valuable, but it is still a wizard explaining another community. Voldemort's explanation of a plan may disclose real actions while misreading loyalty.

## Negative and disputed claims

“No match” means the query found no match. It does not prove that a concept never occurs. Broaden vocabulary, inspect the likely chapter, and distinguish absent exact terminology from absent underlying behaviour. For example, a complete fanfic taxonomy of wards is not provided by the books, but many specific protective enchantments are.

For disagreement, report the smallest defensible conclusion. Avoid inventing a single elegant universal law to remove deliberate ambiguity. If the story needs a choice beyond the text, supply plausible options as inventions and preserve the selected option.

## Useful tool route

Use `hp.mjs facts --query ...` for existing evidence cards; `search --query ... --book ...` for passages; `read --anchor ...` for context. Read output includes continuation offsets. `--full` reads the complete selected chapter. Paths and exact options are in [tools](tools.md).

The answer should remain usable without the local corpus: cite stable chapter anchors and paraphrase the relevant result. Do not embed local private source paths or long excerpts in the public skill.
