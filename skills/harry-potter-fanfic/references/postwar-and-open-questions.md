# Post-war stories and open questions

Use for adult continuations, rebuilding, survival AUs and endings beyond the main narrative.

## Separate established outcomes from open space

DH31–36 establishes major deaths, defeat and immediate choices. The epilogue shows selected family relationships and a school departure nineteen years later. It is not a complete account of employment, law, housing, reform, health or every friendship in between.

Before a post-war scene, confirm who survived under the selected continuity. Dumbledore, Sirius, Fred, Remus, Tonks and Dobby need explicit survival divergences if present alive after their canonical deaths. An AU premise is valid; preserve it and trace its consequences.

The epilogue identifies Neville as a professor connected to Herbology. Do not infer his appointment date or everyone else's career chronology from that fact. Later interviews, articles and stage works are available extension categories, not automatic seven-book evidence.

## Rebuilding as choices

Useful areas for original development include:
- Which institutions regain trust and which procedures actually change.
- Who returns to education, needs work or cannot return home.
- How witnesses, victims, perpetrators and bystanders live near one another.
- Who supplies care and what resources are scarce.
- How memorials represent disagreement as well as shared grief.
- How families renegotiate responsibility after emergency roles end.

These are prompts for invention. The novels do not supply a finished reform programme.

## Relationships over time

Adulthood changes opportunities and obligations; it need not erase earlier habits. Give a returning character an enduring value, a changed strategy and a present pressure. A former rescuer may need to delegate. A meticulous planner may need to share uncertainty. A person seeking status may find a different measure of belonging.

Avoid using trauma as a permanent personality label or a problem cured by romance. People can work, joke and care while still carrying grief. Repair may involve practical restitution and the acceptance that forgiveness is not owed.

## Survivals and alternate endings

A survival changes more than the guest list. Trace housing, advice, inheritance, testimony, loyalties, information and emotional opportunities. If Dobby survives, his own preferences should shape his future. If Remus and Tonks survive, childcare, employment and exposure to prejudice can become active parts of their lives.

Do not preserve a canonical future outcome whose cause was removed unless the AU establishes a new route. Equally, do not assume every divergence changes everything; identify actual dependencies.

## The limits of closure

A peaceful scene can end an arc without resolving every institutional question. Define what has changed for the focal people. A family breakfast might demonstrate a new shared responsibility or an ordinary disagreement that would once have been impossible.

Record invented outcomes in the existing story state so later chapters can build on them. Keep alternative proposals separate until the author chooses.
