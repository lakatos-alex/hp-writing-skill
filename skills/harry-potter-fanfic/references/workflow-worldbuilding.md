# Worldbuilding and institutions

Use for daily life, workplaces, culture, politics, schooling and locations. Build the setting through people doing things under constraints.

## A usable place

Define access, occupants, purpose, authority, resources and rhythms. Who opens the room? Who cleans it? Who may remain after hours? Where does food or equipment come from? Who hears a raised voice? A place becomes distinct through its use.

Canon establishes selected locations and routines, not a complete castle floor plan. Keep invented geography stable within the story. Distinguish a known secret route from an ordinary route and record who has learned it.

## Institutions with competing interests

Separate leadership policy, formal rules, frontline practice and private dissent. Date the scene: the Ministry under denial, open war and capture presents different pressures. A policy can be cruel without every clerk sharing its ideology.

Reform should change a process someone experiences: admissions, wages, access to treatment, custody, evidence handling, employment, representation or reporting. Avoid solving institutional harm solely through one admirable speech or by replacing a villain with a friendly officeholder.

## Labour and material constraints

Household warmth includes work, money, inconvenience and unequal responsibility. Magical labour-saving tools change tasks; they do not eliminate ownership, skill, care or choice. House-elves have individual wants and different responses to their conditions.

Prices anchor a specific purchase, not a complete economy. Use PS5's coin ratios accurately, then label invented wages, budgets or inflation. A post-war shortage needs a cause and an effect on options.

## Culture and Muggle contact

Let a character retain language, food, family practices, professional knowledge and forms of address without making them a walking cultural explanation. Verify specific outside-world facts when precision matters. Mark modern technology and real-world historical research as supplementary evidence, not seven-book canon.

Different generations and households may observe a custom differently. Avoid giving every member of a nationality, house or species the same worldview. Translate or gloss unfamiliar terms only as much as the intended reader needs.

## Design a quiet scene

Choose a practical activity, a relationship pressure and one magical condition that alters the activity. Then let the people disagree or cooperate. A tea scene can contain a refusal, a revelation about finances, or a new division of work. It need not be interrupted by danger to matter.

## Check extensions

Runes as translation study can support an invented technical tradition, but the tradition needs to be labelled and developed. Noble houses, hereditary parliamentary seats and complete magical inheritance law are not supplied by the novels. Use explicit AU choices where the plot needs them.

Deliver only the level needed: a location brief, a set of institutional pressures, a cultural detail integrated into prose, or a broader world bible section in the project's existing format.
