# Locations, access, and everyday Hogwarts life
Use [world foundations](wizarding-world-foundations.md) for broad social dynamics. This reference provides scene logistics and chapter anchors.

## Hogwarts as a place people use
**Arrival and thresholds — PS6–7, PoA5:** railway travel, platform separation, arrival routines and entry into the school make transitions social. Who arrives with friends, who is escorted, who has luggage, who is seen arriving? These questions can carry a scene before any description of towers.

**Great Hall — PS7; GoF16–17:** house tables, staff visibility and public announcements place private reactions in view. Meals can expose a rumour or alter an alliance. Do not assume a character can speak privately across a public table without somebody noticing.

**Gryffindor common room — PS7, PS12:** entry control, dormitories, chairs near the fire, homework and holiday quiet shape belonging. Use occupancy: a crowded room at evening differs from the same chairs during the Christmas break. Other houses have their own arrangements; do not generalise Gryffindor's entrance mechanism to all four.

**Library — PS12–13; CoS10:** research has access, time and social constraints. Restricted books are a reason to negotiate or break rules. One conveniently discovered paragraph should not resolve a mystery before the story has earned its interpretation.

**Classrooms and laboratories — PS8; PoA7; HBP9:** instruction has demonstrations, mistakes, deadlines, equipment, ingredients, marks and embarrassment. A specialist's classroom should reveal what that specialist notices, not merely the name of the subject.

**Kitchens — GoF21:** the fruit picture and pear give a concrete entrance; house-elves work below the public dining space. Dining abundance has labour behind it. Ask who requested food, who made it and what relationship gives a visitor access.

**Room of Requirement — OotP18; HBP21; DH29:** seventh-floor corridor, intent and repeated passage provide discovery and access. Different uses do not all become available simultaneously. The room responds to need but is not a device for omniscience or creating food. DH29's connection to the Hog's Head is a specific solution, not a general castle teleporter.

**Hospital wing — CoS10; PoA21:** recovery interrupts plans. Madame Pomfrey controls rest and visitors; classmates may misunderstand absence. Injured people can have agency without leaping immediately back into battle.

**Grounds, greenhouses and hut — PS15; PoA6; GoF26:** weather, daylight, distance, mud and living creatures constrain movement. Herbology gives practical knowledge; Hagrid's hospitality can be sincere while his risk judgement fails. The lake is habitat, not just atmosphere or a blank swimming pool.

**Forbidden Forest — PS15; OotP33:** different beings have different loyalties, territories and judgments. An armed school party does not own the forest. A creature's hostility can be about intrusion rather than innate evil.

**Passages and the Shrieking Shack — PoA10, PoA17–19:** routes have histories and users. The Shack's reputation conceals a protective arrangement. A map is incomplete if people can change or discover access routes.
**Chamber of Secrets — CoS16–17:** entrance conditions and historical concealment matter. Knowing a chamber exists is not equivalent to being able to reach or use it.
**Astronomy Tower — HBP27:** height and constrained exits make confrontation physically legible. Track who can move, who is immobilised and who can hear activity below.

## Hogsmeade
**PoA8–10:** permission and age/year eligibility make visiting a privilege with consequences. Harry's concealed visits are meaningful because access is restricted.
**Three Broomsticks:** social warmth and mixed adult company allow information to be overheard; overhearing must have a plausible position and motive.
**Hog's Head — OotP16; DH28–29:** apparent marginality, surveillance and later refuge coexist. A meeting in an obscure pub is not automatically secret.
**Honeydukes:** pleasure, purchasing power and a concealed route make a shop more than a sweets inventory.
For an adult story, school permission does not govern every adult visitor, but school security and employment arrangements still need definition.

## London and the wider community
**Diagon Alley — PS5:** money exchange, school supplies, wand fitting and professional shops introduce stratification through a shopping list. Knowledge of the entrance is an access advantage.
**Knockturn Alley — CoS4; HBP6:** adjacency to ordinary shopping matters. Suspect trade is embedded in society rather than a separate evil dimension.
**Ministry — OotP7–8; DH12–13:** entrances, reception, lifts, departments and legal proceedings let authority operate spatially. A captured institution may preserve familiar furniture while changing who is allowed through it.
**St Mungo's — OotP22–23:** concealed public entry, wards and visitors join secrecy to ordinary care. The closed ward demonstrates long-term harm and family lives that continue outside the protagonist's plot.
**Gringotts — PS5; DH26:** service, expertise and vault security have political owners. A withdrawal and a break-in should not use the same frictionless access.
**Privet Drive — PS1–4; OotP1–2:** tidy surfaces can coexist with cruelty and surveillance. Avoid substituting a generic gothic prison for the specific importance of appearances.
**The Burrow — CoS3; GoF5; DH6–8:** household work, noise, finances and guests create warmth. Crowding generates both intimacy and lack of privacy.
**Grimmauld Place — OotP4–6; DH10:** concealment and safety coexist with inherited hostility. Cleaning changes space through labour, not only a decorative mood.
**Godric's Hollow — DH16–17:** memorial, home, public history and danger intersect. Grief affects what visitors notice and which precautions they neglect.
**Shell Cottage — DH24–25:** refuge is recovery plus decisions and hospitality work. Its peace is not proof that the war has stopped.
**Malfoy Manor — DH23:** a familiar family space becoming a site of captivity reveals how political allegiance enters a home.

## Daily-life planning grid
For a quiet scene choose: one practical task, one relationship change, and one world detail that affects the task. For example, a borrowed book cannot leave the library; a visitor must return before curfew; an owl arrives during an awkward breakfast. Do not insert every detail in this reference into a single paragraph.

Check term/year, timetable, exams, holidays, clothes, supplies, sleep, travel and money only to the depth the scene needs. The novels leave many exact timetables and room positions unspecified. Invent consistently and label invention; avoid claiming a definitive castle floor plan.

## Book/film separation
Do not assume films establish book facts. Book Ravenclaw uses an eagle; film merchandising commonly promotes a raven. Dobby provides the Room lead in OotP18. Book resolution of the Elder Wand differs from the film image of snapping it. Verify architecture and character actions against the selected medium.
